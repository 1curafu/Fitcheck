I want to go with the second argument.

The key point is that we should not treat “navy-on-navy tonal dressing” and “a navy accent echoing a navy garment” as the same mechanism.

For example, navy top + navy pants is tonal dressing. That should remain a separate concept.

But navy top + white pants + white Forces with a navy swoosh is different. The navy swoosh is a small visual accent on an otherwise white sneaker, and it is clearly picking up the navy from the top. From a normal human styling perspective, that is an intentional colour echo. If our system ignores it simply because navy is classified as neutral, that is exactly the original bug.

The same problem applies to brown, camel, tan, and denim. These colours are correctly treated as neutral in the overall menswear palette, but they can still function as accents on a specific item. For example, a small brown detail on a white sneaker can intentionally echo a brown jacket, even though brown is a neutral palette colour.

So I do NOT want to reclassify navy, brown, camel, tan, or denim as accent colours globally. Their neutral classification should remain because it is important for the palette logic and the 3-colour ceiling.

Instead, the system should distinguish between the colour’s palette classification and its role on the specific item.

In other words:

* navy can remain a neutral palette colour.
* A navy swoosh on a white sneaker can still have the semantic role of an accent.
* That accent should be able to echo a navy garment and receive the appropriate reward.

Please implement this as a general rule rather than a special case for navy.

The important distinction is:

navy garment + navy garment → tonal dressing

navy garment + white sneaker with navy accent → accent echo

The second case must not become invisible just because navy is classified as neutral. if it goes against rules of research say me and go discuss with me
